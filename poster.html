Introduction

  • Linguistic Complexity: Unlike simple keyword-based systems, this project utilizes a non-trivial customer support dataset with high vocabulary overlap and intentional noise (typos, mixed intents).
  • Classical vs Dense Models: Evaluates the performance of classical Machine Learning architectures against advanced dense embeddings in the context of automated support triage.
  • The Challenge: Automated systems require more than accurate labels; they need mathematical certainty (calibrated confidence) to know when to automate and when to escalate to humans.

Dataset Profile

  • Composition: Analyzed over 500 real-world customer support tickets spanning multiple priority tiers.
  • Characteristics: The dataset is highly representative of production environments, containing organic spelling errors, overlapping technical terminology, and ambiguous user phrasing.

SCOPE of the Project

  • Automated Routing & Priority: Escalating High-confidence Critical tickets while flagging ambiguous ones for human review.
  • Robust Feature Engineering: Evaluating TF-IDF sparse vectors against S-BERT (MiniLM) and Fine-tuned Transformer (DistilBERT) embeddings.
  • Resource Efficiency: Prioritizing models with <5ms inference time compared to resource-heavy Deep Learning models (>100ms).
  • Calibrated Ensemble Construction: Developing a soft voting classifier that leverages complementary strengths of Support Vector Machines and Logistic Regression.

The process consists of two main steps:

  • Step A: Feature Representation (TF-IDF)
    • Extracts informative urgency tokens by balancing term frequency with document sparsity.
    • Formula: W(t,d) = TF(t,d) × log(N / DF(t))
  • Step B: Calibrated Ensemble Architecture
    • A Soft Voting classifier aggregates the probability predictions of SVM and Logistic Regression.
    • Formula: ŷ = argmax Σ wj Pj(y|x)

  • Orthogonal Feature Dominance: Sparse, high-dimensional TF-IDF vectors explicitly capture technical "urgency tokens" (e.g., "latency", "handshake"), significantly outperforming the smoothed continuous representations of S-BERT.
  • Inference Latency Trade-off: The proposed Calibrated Ensemble performs inference in <5ms on a single CPU core, achieving a 95% latency reduction compared to Transformer architectures (>100ms), which is critical for high-throughput real-time triage.
  • Stability & Convergence: Learning curve diagnostics confirm that the 5-fold cross-validation scores converge monotonically with the training scores, indicating that the model generalizes robustly without high variance (overfitting).
  • Auditability Constraints: Unlike black-box neural networks, the linear classifiers provide explicit feature importance coefficients, satisfying corporate requirements for transparent, interpretable triage logic.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that for domain-specific, linguistically complex customer support environments, a calibrated classical ensemble (SVM + Logistic Regression) with TF-IDF representations significantly outperforms modern Deep Learning alternatives. By providing mathematically sound confidence scores and maintaining <5ms inference latency, the proposed architecture offers a highly interpretable, production-ready solution for reliable "Human-in-the-loop" automated ticket triage.
